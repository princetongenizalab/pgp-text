Recto

The letter of [my lord, the shaykh … may God prolong his life,] arrived,


and may He perpetuate his glory and blessings, and never deprive him of His gracious [guidance … and may He be for him]


a protector and guardian in all his affairs. I congratulate [him] on these


blessed festivals. May God cause them to return upon him for many years, with an abundance of [...]


and may He reunite us with him in the best of circumstances, by His grace and subtle kindness, God willing.


What he wishes to know is that I have investigated what you instructed me concerning, namely the news of Maghreb.


As for al-Mahdiyya, it remains in a state of safety. It was reported that two ships had departed from it:


one of them a ship belonging to the ruler of al-Mahdiyya, and the other a Byzantine ship. They also reported


that its prices are those of al-saqaṭ [?], namely good and profitable prices,


unlike al-Mariyya. As for Tripoli, after [...] there came concerning it the report


which was concealed from no one. A trustworthy Muslim man was captured/taken, and he reported news that


matters had been resolved and that it had returned to Muslim control. We hope that this is indeed true by God’s will.


Your servant had sent [something] in the care of Abū l-Ḥasan, and I ask your favor that


you send me some goods, among them a small quantity of brazilwood, at whatever price, provided that it is of good quality. If


it is convenient to send something in his care, [do so]; otherwise send me one dinar’s worth quickly, for I am without it.


Your illustrious presence is especially greeted with the fullest peace. To Shaykh Abū ʿAlī, the choicest greetings, and to his children,


greetings; and to my lord, the shaykh and raʾīs, the choicest greetings, and greetings to all those encompassed by his care.

Right margin
