Verso, left, main text

Praise be to God.


In the law court referenced on the recto,


Abū al-Baqāʾ and Abū al-ʿIzz, mentioned on the recto, pledged to the possession of Muʿallim Joseph,


mentioned on the recto, by way of security for the amount mentioned on the recto and for every part of it until


the time of its legal repayment, that which is in their hands and in their possession, and the two of them testified to this with their acknowledgement


before the [lit., ‘its’] witnesses, namely, the entire share whose quantity is the half and the quarter,


18 full shares of the entirety of the boat that customarily sails in the lakes of


Tinnīs, which is described [as follows:]—its height from its base is eleven cubits,


made fast with ropes [?], with a tiller with a rudder [?], caulked [?] at the hull. A portion of the aforementioned share [consists]


of its equipment, namely, a mast to which are attached a ballast of sea water and a lamp and sails [?]


and pieces/units of bolts [?] and //rigging [?]// and an anchor made of nail-iron and four oars in ...


and four punting-poles and a quadrant [?] and a compass [?] on which are [inscribed] the [cardinal] directions and the surroundings


and the wind currents, known to them by legal knowledge—a sound, legal, accepted, received pledge,


delivered with permission, customary,


for benefit from it through the transfer of authority over the aforementioned pledge according to his [the judge’s] ruling on it,


with their acknowledgement of that before the [lit., ‘its’] witnesses and their turning it over to him [Joseph]. When this was completed


with their testimony, as explained above, and there was certified before our lord the humble slave


to God the exalted, the shaykh, the imam, the know- ledgeable, the erudite, the majesty of religion,


glory of the ʿulamāʾ, unique among the favoured, the indisputable mufti, Abī al-Baqāʾ Muḥammad al-Dumyāṭī


al-Shāfīʿī, the noble deputy judge in the port mentioned on the recto, may God the exalted support his judgements


and favour him—those mentioned above testifying for themselves according to what is attributed to them above


in the text and language above, the certification according to attested testimony—


he ruled, may God the exalted support his judgements and favour him, according to


the testimony of each of them—a sound, legal,


complete, considered, satisfactory, responsible ruling, fulfilling the written and claimed conditions


of the sharīʿa, cognisant of the [potential for a] contradictory [ruling] on it. He bore witness


his noble self to this on the date on the recto after the passing of what was specified on the recto.

Verso, right

Praise to God, the lord of the universe.


In the law court in the Musallamiyya Madrasa in the port of Damietta


the well-protected in justice, Muʿallim Joseph,


mentioned on the recto, acknowledged before the [lit., ‘its’]
witnesses, that the amount named on the recto,


whose total, in the silver coins described on the recto, is
1,000 niṣf [fiḍḍa]


and 500 niṣfs, belongs to his father, the aforementioned
Abraham,


rather than to him [i.e., Joseph] or anyone else, as he acknowledged to the [lit., ‘its’] witnesses.


Muʿallim Yaʿqūb [i]bn Yūsuf the Rabbanite Jew, the scribe
of the dīwān in the port,


and the legal agent on behalf of Abraham Castro, mentioned on the recto, purchased with the money of his aforementioned employer [lit., the one who made him agent] from the aforementioned two young men [?]


mentioned on the recto, namely, Abū al-Baqāʾ and Abū al- ʿIzz, mentioned on the recto, undertaking


the sale, to be mentioned in it [the record], on their own behalf and on behalf of the other [?] heirs of their father, mentioned on the recto:


the two of them sold it to his [Yaʿqūb’s] employer, the aforementioned Abraham, that which was in their possession and in the possession of their father’s heirs and theirs [the heirs’] to dispose of,


with the acknowledgement of the two of them before the [lit., ‘its’] witnesses, and it [i.e., what is in their possession] is all of the share amounting to the half and the quarter, 18


complete shares of the entirety of the boat that customarily sails in the lakes of Tinnīs and vicinity,


with its equipment, specified on the left, known to them by legal knowledge ... a legal purchase


for a price whose total is in the transaction completed in
large Ottoman silver pieces: 1,000 niṣf [fiḍḍa] and 100 niṣfs


and sixty-five niṣfs in current cash. A release of liability was
granted from him, a legal release by the legal path


by the necessity of a legal agreement regarding what the aforementioned Abraham Castro is entitled to from the two aforementioned brothers,


sons of Badr al-Dīn, named on the recto, the legal agreement in the legal path, and that is by legal agreement


that occurred between them in this, by offer and receipt and legal delivery. The entire aforementioned


boat and its equipment came to be shared between the aforementioned Abraham and Sulaymān [i]bn Abī al-Ḥasan


al-Jarbalī the boatman, specifically, as explained in it [the document], that which belongs to the aforementioned Abraham from


the share specified above, and that which belongs to the aforementioned Sulaymān, a share amounting to one quarter of the boat and its equipment,


specified on the left. The aforementioned Muʿallim Yaʿqūb acknowledged his release of the two aforementioned brothers on behalf of his aforementioned employer [i.e., Abraham],


from the remainder of the amount named on the recto, the legal release by the legal path, a release of legal taking possession and receiving payment,


legally, and nothing remained to him [Abraham] of that, not a little and not much. Then the aforementioned Yaʿqūb acknowledged, and he by himself


is the first party in what will be mentioned in it [the acknowledgement], and the aforementioned two brothers, and they are the second party in the legal acknowledge- ment,


that neither of the two parties deserves or is entitled to [anything] from the other party. The aforementioned Yaʿqūb added, in


his acknowledgement, that his aforementioned employer [i.e., Abraham] has no claim against Budayr and his father, [no] right, no entitlement,


no claim, no demand in any manner or [for any] reason, not in silver, gold, or copper,


not by seizure or confiscation, not by account or by an error in one, not in ignorance or forgetfulness, not


in absentmindedness, not by coercion or claim to it, not in valuables and not by liability, not by swearing by God the exalted,


nothing, not a little nor much, from previous times to the present day. He [Yaʿqūb] enjoined Abū al-Baqāʾ and Abū al-ʿIzz, mentioned


above, in their possessions and in their money, that if a legal claimant should sue Yaʿqūb or his aforementioned employer [i.e., Abraham]


for any financial right pertaining to the share mentioned above, and if this should be proven against both of them or against either one of them with a legal proof,


and if he [the hypothetical claimant] should compel him [Yaʿqūb or Abraham] to pay, and he paid, then it was incumbent on both of them to repay him [Yaʿqūb or Abraham] all that he had paid, no matter the amount—a legal enjoining,


accepted with their acknowledgement of this before the [lit., ‘its’] witnesses and their agreement upon the legal agreement.


Our lord [?, i.e., the judge] bore witness to the granting of power of attorney in the ... and the judgement on it was on the second of the month of Jumādā [al-]Awwal


of the year 945 [26 September 1538].

Recto, main text

In the name of God, the merciful, the compassionate. Trust [in God] [?].


In the law court at the Musallamiyya madrasa in the port of Damietta the well-protected, before our master, the poor slave in need of God the exalted, the shaykh, the most evident imam,


the knowledgeable, the erudite, the ornament of religion, the glory of the ʿulamāʾ, unique among the favoured, the indisputable mufti, Abī Hurayra ʿAbd al-Raḥman


al-Aḳṣarāyī the Ḥanbalī, the great deputy judge in the aforementioned port, may God the exalted support his judgements and favour him with probity.


The two full brothers Abū al-Baqāʾ and Abū al-ʿIzz, sons of the late Badr al-Dīn, [also] known as Budayr, son of the late al-Shihābī


Aḥmad, [also] known as Bīghos (Bey) al-Ḥaramī, their grandfather, in Ghayd al-Naṣāra in the aforementioned port, and Muʿallim Joseph the son of Muʿallim Abraham Castro,


the current ʿāmil of the aforementioned port, who is trusted by the two of them, reached a legal agreement while in a state of health, well-being, free will, and choice, that that to which


the aforementioned Muʿallim Joseph is entitled—that is owed by Abī al-Baqāʾ and Abī al-ʿIzz, mentioned above, of [?]...the legal tax farm


to their aforementioned father Budayr, or to their aforementioned grandfather Aḥmad Bīghos, of legal debts prior to this date and from enterprises among them—


totals, in large Ottoman silver coins current in Egypt now, 1000 niṣf [fiḍḍa] and 500 niṣf legal liability,


by legal means, by way of exigible debt. Abū al-Baqāʾ and Abū al-ʿIzz, mentioned above, and they are the first party in what is to be mentioned,


and the aforementioned Muʿallim Joseph, and he is by himself the second party to the legal settlement, acknowledged in a legal acknowledgement that neither party


is entitled to [anything] or has any right to [anything] from the other after this. The aforementioned Muʿallim Joseph added to his acknowledgement that he was not


entitled, on his own behalf or that of his father the aforementioned Abraham, [to anything more] from the first party or from the debt of the aforementioned Budayr or the debt of his father


Aḥmad Bīghos or any of their heirs, [no] right, no entitlement, no claim, no demand in any manner or [for any] reason,


not in silver, gold, or copper, not by seizure or confiscation, not by account or by an error in one, not in ignorance or


forgetfulness or absentmindedness, not through debt or in cash, not by testament, not by borrowing, not through coercion, no claim to this,


nothing at all, a little or much, great or humble, blameworthy or trustworthy, directly or through an agent [?], fixed


or mobile, of whatever type or kind or description, not in valuables and not by liability, not by swearing


by God the exalted, nothing, not a little nor much, from previous times to the present day, except for what was owed to the aforementioned Joseph by Abī al-Baqāʾ


and Abī al-ʿIzz, mentioned above, and that is the amount agreed to above and the items of pledge that will be enumerated on the right of the verso,


which he pledged today to his heirs on that basis. Each party released the debt, hand, and pledge of the other


for all the valuables, liabilities, claims, demands, and obligatory oaths—a legal, definitive, firm, final release,


...overriding all rights, claims, and oaths before this date and until this date, except for those named above. Each of the two parties accepted.
