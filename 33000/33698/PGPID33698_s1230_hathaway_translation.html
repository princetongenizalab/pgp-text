English Translation of Literary text: ENA 3904.13

Jane Hathaway, Ottoman-Era Documents from the Cairo Genizah (Open Book Publishers, 2026).

Recto, right column

  1. [Rubric:] ʿĀşıḳ ʿÖmer

  2. First of all, we are faithful and keep the covenant of ‘they said, “Of course!’’’.

  3. We do not conform to the words of the ignorant; with intellect, we have quick understanding.

  4. I have fallen into exile. Give me my desire, o God.

  5. We are in a bad time when the promises of the great are not kept.

  6. If you are intelligent, grasp the skirt of the [mystical] guide.

  7. Be a guest for many years in his [the guide’s] Sufi lodge and sleep there.

  8. Load up your goods here; sell them there.

  9. We are in a great caravan that never stops but goes on by.

  10. Our [spiritual] state is known to the intelligent ʿāşıḳs [Sufi masters].

  11. O God! Make easy our questions in the grave.

  12. When a thousand and one feet become one, what will be-

    come of us?

  13. We conform to Satan with our souls and rebel with our sins.

  14. ʿÖmer [the poet] says, go to other than the beloved for the sake of the beloved.

  15. ʿÖmer says, take the ʿāşıḳ’s words.

  16. If you are afflicted, o heart, find a remedy for your pain.

  17. Perform pure ablutions and pray [at the prescribed] five times.

  18. Any day now, we will be in a great court.

  19. Wake from this heedless dream! We are in a great court.

  20. [Rubric:] ʿĀşıḳ ʿÖmer

  21. The beloved has pulled his dagger of reproach on us now and forevermore.

  22. ‘It turns out there is no truth in you. Go away forevermore’ [quoting the beloved].

  23. I have no strength left for patience, for no time is left to me.

  24. The wind has thrown my heart into the wilderness forever- more.

  25. I burble like an overflowing stream.

  26. I will give up everything [lit., ‘remove my clothes’] and, like a dervish, don the sheepskin [lit., ‘attach the sheepskin to my back’].

  27. What shall I do with the beloved who does not keep his word?

  28. I have given up [vain] perseverance, honour, and shame forevermore.

  29. Tell my rose-faced shah:

  30. I did not know your worth; don’t look at my sin [i.e., for- give me].

  31. Thus since that moment I have been in the orbit of [lit., ‘have performed service for’] my shah whose name is Meḥmed,

  32. My intelligence has reached the point that I am confused forever //more//.

Recto, left column

  1. 1. ʿÖmer [the poet] says, turn to other than the beloved for the sake of the beloved.

  2. 2. ʿÖmer says, don’t do favours for others for the sake of the beloved.

  3. 3. It doesn’t matter. Don’t do favours for the thorn for the sake of the rose.

  4. 4. Don’t do favours for a thorn who doesn’t know the value of things.

  5. I am leaving this land, may it be ḥarām, forevermore. 

  6. [Rubric:] Yaḥyā Çavuş

  7. O God, make whoever despises us great and mighty.

  8. Let us be disregarded; let them [i.e., the people who despise us] be respected. 

  9. Whoever may have remembered us [as having] despicable

    morals in [this] world,

  10. May he always be distinguished by good character and em- inence.

  11. [As long as we are] in the universe, we are among the lost, o Creator.

  12. May whoever mentions us live forever.

  13. Whoever angrily wants us to be unsettled in [this] world,

  14. May God be generous to him and always let him be settled.

  15. May whoever wants us always to be in poverty and abase- ment

  16. Exist as long as the universe for the sake of almighty God’s truth.

  17. Whoever says of us, ‘They can’t find a place among the peo- ple of calamity’,

  18. May happiness give him honey; may he be one of God’s kings.

  19. Those who want us to be grief-stricken and sad at all times,

  20. May they always be happy and joyful, may their every day be springtime. 

  21. Whoever reproaches us with misfortune in [this] world,

  22. May his every moment in the universe be abundant with glory and good fortune.

  23. Whoever talks behind our backs and speaks of our flaws in [this] world,

  24. May there be [i.e., may he experience] countless echoes from the divine hidden world.

  25. Whoever wants us not to get our desire in these times—

  26. May I not get what I want; only let them get their desires.

  27. Whoever curses us to our faces, o God, every moment,

  28. May our prayers and praise be a souvenir from us to him.

  29. No one has property and fortune, silver and gold, forever.

  30. The real art is to repair a heart in ruins. 

Recto, lower right margin, in red ink

  1. [by] Yaḥyā Çavuş//[in] the year 1172 [1758]
