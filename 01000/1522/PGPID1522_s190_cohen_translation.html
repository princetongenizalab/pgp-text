English Translation of Letter: T-S 10J16.4

Mark R. Cohen, The Voice of the Poor in the Middle Ages (Princeton University Press, 2005).

  1. Your slave, the wife (= widow) of 
  2. [the] cantor Ben Naḥman 
  3. Your slave informs your excellency the Gaon, may your r[ul]e be established forever,
  4. [about the situati]on of your slave and her son, their hunger and nakedness. That is because your slave
  5. has been banished, she and her son, from her house for three months, without being able
  6. to enter it. For whenever I enter the house,
  7. Bayan, the son of my husband, pounces upon your slave and inflicts pain on me in my most
  8. vital organs and vulnerable places, intending thereby
  9. to harm me. The Iast // [t]ime // your slave left the house,
  10. had not a Turk happened to be passing by, he would
  11. have killed me. I st[r]etched out my hand and had just begun to cry out for help
  12. when he happened by. Also, he (her stepson) and his aunt have banded together
  13. against me and concocted improper things about me. The reason for your slave's forbearance
  14. all this time until today has been the fear lest, God forbid, the bo[y]
  15. go astray and it be blamed on me. Otherwise your slave would
  16. have s[o]ught the help of God the ex(alted) and of your noble gate. [But] now
  17. the knif[e] has reached the bone and your slave app[eals] for help to [God]
  18. the ex(alted) and your noble gate, to be shown favor [and]
  19. have her case considered and have pity shown on her. I have a right in that house,
  20. praised be God. If your slave does not trust in God the ex(alted), I shall go
  21. my way. May t(he) h(oly one) b(lessed be) H(e) double your reward and be [your] succor.
