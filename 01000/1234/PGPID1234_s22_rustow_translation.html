Unspecified (Unpublished Transcriptions) Translation of Letter: T-S 13J9.2

Marina Rustow, unpublished.

Reco

  1. To our beloved, our companion, our honored and precious one, (his) h(onor), g(reatness, and) h(oliness), our teacher and master Efrayim the Ḥaver—may he grow mighty in stronghold and refuge,
  2. may he lodge in the shadow of the Almighty and prevail, may he cleave to and join with those who fear God, may he rule by his spirit,
  3. overcome amidst every adversary who transgresses and rages, so that through him the name of the pronouncer of righteousness may be loved,
  4. may his well-being increase and blessings prevail, may every desire and hope be fulfilled for him—son of Shemarya (may his) s(oul find) r(est).
  5. These lines have been written to our companion—may the Rock be his support—out of heartache and a broken spirit on account of
  6. the troubles in which we are sunk and the misfortunes in which we are submerged. Day and night we are fearful,
  7. trembling due to the encamped armies. May our Rock strengthen the camps of our lord the caliph, may he live forever. Thus
  8. do we continually offer prayer for their support, so that they may prevail over those from the East who have laid waste to the Land of Beauty (cf. Daniel 11:16). 
  9. Roads are desolate and the wayfarer has ceased to travel. For those going and coming, there is no peace — until the Gracious and Merciful One shows mercy and compassion.
  10. It would have been enough for us to bear the upheavals that have sapped our strength, dimmed our sight, buckled our knees,
  11. melted our hearts, and grieved our souls. But in addition to all this, harsh reports reached us concerning
  12. the communities (qehillot) of Egypt: they have split into factions (kitot), one faction saying one thing and another saying another, each of them
  13. passing judgment in letters that arrive bearing the names of people some of whom we know and some of whom we do
  14. not know, some of whose words are couched in ethical reproof and some in language of grievance. They complain and say
  15. that most of the people wished to lodge a complaint against me before the government (malkhut), may God protect it, because I authorized a man who is unworthy.
  16. They defame you with evil words. May the Alm(ighty) exact judgment from anyone who defames others and visit upon him
  17. a cruel verdict. They say that only on account of your gifts and your tribute do our letters come to you,
  18. so that you gain support from them against them (your enemies). But God was there (i.e., only God knows the truth). We were very much and exceedingly distressed by everything written
  19. in their letters. Indeed, all of this is from the hand of the one whom we suspect incites people to form factions (kitot), to terrify
  20. and to intimidate, just like the deeds of Sanballaṭ and Tobias {Nehemiah 2:19 ff.). May God strike him for his sin, and may his transgression not be blotted out before God. 
  21. A letter had previously been sent to you together with the government decree (cf. Ezra 4:7) written to the communities making peace, to bless them all collectively
  22. and to express gratitude to whoever conducted himself well, whoever made an effort, and whoever pursued peace, bestowing upon them additional
  23. blessings. We had assumed that the dispute had already subsided. But now everything has returned,
  24. the dispute has intensified, the quarreling has grown, and the outcry has become great. There are alarming letters from certain people —
  25. a testimony with witnesses' signatures on it whose signatories attest that everyone has consented to Mar Rav
  26. Shemuʾel ha-Kohen son of Avṭalyon, (may his) s(oul find) r(est), to serve as the overseer over them, to adjudicate their cases and judge
  27. their disputes. Agony was added upon grief: the suspect also goes about among the elders picking quarrels and says,
  28. "Look at the letter of so-and-so — he has reviled the earlier ones," claiming that the former heads (rashim), each and
  29. every one of them, sought to shed his fellow's blood. He also reviled R. Elḥanan, saying, "Consider this deed
  30. and the deed of the transgressor." Indeed these are the words of all (i.e., everyone's versions of events agree), testifying to me that it was so. The communities of Egypt,
  31. may God be their help, know all about these disputes and quarrels, which have reached the point of endangering lives, and (know) that the transgressor's
  32. eyes and heart were not (in the right place?) but only on whoever would feed him and give him drink and fulfill his heart's desire. There is none who seeks (the truth?) and none who inquires.
  33. Their souls are not inclined toward those who reprove. Is it not said among the Fathers, "They lay snares for the reprover at the gate, and with nothingness they turn aside
  34. the righteous" (Isaiah 29:21; Amos 5:10)? Who is the one who exercised authority over a house of substance and reproved them and they loved him for it? Did not
  35. Micah say, "Do not preach"? They preach! "Do not preach to these; disgrace shall not overtake [them]" (Micah 2:6) — the meaning of which is: they say to the preachers,
  36. "Do not preach! Do not be preachers of your prophecies to this people!" And "But if a man walking
  37. in wind and falsehood should lie, preaching of wine and strong drink, he would be acceptable to this people" (Micah 2:11).
  38. Moreover, to God himself, did we not [say], "We did not listen," and to His prophecies, "We did not pay heed," and with His messengers we mocked and trifled and rebelled?
  39. His precious words we treated as nothing. If even the prophets had power to do no more than warn, "the one who listens
  40. will listen and the one who refuses will refuse" (Ezekiel 3:27): From the day He chose us, He called us a stiff-necked people. He is able to change
  41. our inclination, but He has left us as He created us, and set before us the way of good and the way of evil, to see which is
  42. the good way in which to walk until the time He has promised to circumcise our hearts and remove the heart of stone from our flesh
  43. and give us a heart of flesh (Ezekiel 36:26). You, our companion, should know that the people of God at this time — their injury is grievous, their wound is
  44. sore. Thorns have multiplied among them and we have no strength to destroy them. Leave it to the Owner of the vineyard: He will destroy his own thorns. 
  45. Do not give the slanderers an opening to defame (you) and say, "Before he investigates the affairs of others, let him investigate
  46. himself and heal the \\debts// that are upon him." God forbid a man like you should furnish such a pretext. Act so that the name of
  47. Heaven is beloved through you. If, by the mercies of Heaven — \\I trust// in your diligence that the matter is not so: if concerning God
  48. himself they have fabricated things that are not true, how much more so concerning His creatures? From the documents that
  49. were returned to Egypt, you will observe who did this, for they have acted insolently and spoken boastfully. It has been confirmed that some of them
  50. are intoxicated (with alcohol or with their own arrogance?). There is a Judge and there is justice. Although my heart urges me to pronounce a ban (lehaḥrim) in the Name (of God) and to proclaim
  51. this deed's wickedness amidst the congregation of the Lord, I turn back and say: What use would it be? Rather, at the gates I cast
  52. my supplication, to call upon God in cae there is [someone willing] to seek redress for the aggrieved, to silence the mouths of the slanderers, to put to shame
  53. those who spread slander. I have trusted in God and in Him I have taken refuge. If the hand of the slanderers is raised and
  54. the arm of the disputants has prevailed, it is in their power to set up over themselves as head and chief whomever is good in their eyes,
  55. to turn the people into the priest (cf. Isaiah 24:2), to judge their own judges. I sit in the City of the Sanctuary (i.e., Jerusalem),
  56. may God reestablish it, weeping over myself and over the time that has need of me. I am not worthy
  57. for the people of this age. But what is to be done? The Name has been invoked (i.e., the office of gaʾon has been conferred by divine providence), and it is impossible to reject what
  58. He has bestowed. May His name be blessed. My heart has not grown proud, nor have my eyes been haughty. I walk in my integrity; my Creator
  59. has raised me up in His goodness. If He should say, "I have no delight in you," lo, I am here; let Him do to me what is fitting in His eyes.
  60. I have informed you of the bitterness they make me drink on your account. I have already written to some of them to reprove them.
  61. May the King of Glory give you rest from all your enemies round about. May your peace and the peace of your entire community increase forever.
  62. Shelomo the lowly (literally, "the young," ha-ṣaʿir), Head of the Yeshiva of the Pride of Jacob, son of rabbi [Yehuda]

Recto, right margin

  1. A letter has already been sent from me to my lord the elder Abū ʿAlī Ḥasan ibn al-Ṭayyib, may God support him,
  2. containing (information) that will dispel (rumors) of what has been said to him, (namely) that he has been dismissed, and [assuring him] that he holds great honor with me. Let him set his mind at ease.
  3. Convey to him special greetings. Peace.

Verso, address

  1. To the precious glory, our companion and our beloved, (his) h(onor), g(reatness, and) h(oliness), son of Rabbi Shemarya the teacher, may he rest in Eden,

  2. our master and teacher Efrayim the Ḥaver, may our Rock preserve him. Great salvation.
