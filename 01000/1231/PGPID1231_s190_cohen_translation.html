English Translation of Letter: T-S 13J9.11

Mark R. Cohen, The Voice of the Poor in the Middle Ages (Princeton University Press, 2005).

Recto

  1. Your s[la]ve kisses t[h]e ground [be]fore the excellent [seat of our lord],
  2. may it be uplifted and increase and may you live forever, and informs you that I had presented
  3. before your excellent seat, may it be uplifted, a petition containing
  4. a partial explanation of my situation. One of the students of our lord came forth (from the capital, apparently) and told
  5. your servant (i.e., the writer) on behalf of our master that you would not be ready to execute
  6. the matter under after the holiday. You had earlier generously promised
  7. to execute the matter as it was known to you. Your servant is
  8. in a desperate situation in all his affairs and I ask you
  9. in your munificence not to neglect me but to pay full attention to my weak state (meaning, poverty)
  10. and desolation. The knife has reached
  11. the bone. My greatest adversity is in the matter of this creditor
  12. and the fact that he is one of the better people, so that he is justified and I am
  13. the one who wrongs him and myself. I do not blame him, for I have fallen in with him
  14. because of my many sins. The purpose of this (petition) is that when
  15. my master meets with the illustrious head, our master and teacher R. Abraham the great Rav, may his glory be exalted
  16. and his honor increased, you, my master, should tell him what you know
  17. to be the truth of your servant’s situation and ask him to procure
  18. 60 dirhems in any way possible and send for
  19. the creditor and ask him to divide the sum into installments of 10
  20. dirhems per month. That is what he requested, since he knows (my)
  21. situation. He is well aware of my extreme predicament. The writ in his hand
  22. divides it into 20 dirhems per month. Let it be promised to him
  23. that the 60 dirhams be paid in six monthly installments. If
  24. that is done, it will be help from God by the merit
  25. of our lord. The matter is easy. The matter can be paid off by the end of
  26. Passover, God willing, with the help of our lord and by the merit
  27. of his father, (may) the m(emory of) the r(ighteous be) b(lessed). Your servant has informed you of this after having kissed
  28. your feet. May the welfare of his excellence our holy lord,
  29. increase forever and never diminish. Forever, sela amen.

 

Verso

  1. Concerning Reuben against whom there is a bi[ill of debt…,]
  2. and he lacks the means to pay and does not have that with which to support
  3. himself or his family to the extent that, were he able, he would swear
  4. to his fellow litigant with a solemn oath that he has nothing and is without
  5. means. And some of the food and furnishings are not designated for him
  6. but for others. And his opposing party is oppressing him and summons him
  7. to a gentile (i.e., Muslim) court. If he admits it, he (the creditor) may become violent
  8. and have him arrested. And he does not heed the ruling of Jewish law.
  9. If he denies it, the qāḍī may require of him an oath,
  10. and he is afraid of the (heavenly) punishment. May you instruct us: if the violating opposing party
  11. transgresses and summons him to a gentile court, is he allowed
  12. to swear that he does not owe him anything in this case
  13. and be virtuous or not? And being well
  14. aware of the situation of the debtor and knowing the truth of
  15. his destitution, may the judge send after the violent opposing party and tell him,
  16. “if you summon him to a gentile court, you will no longer have any claim
  17. on him and you will be obligated such and such by Jewish law”? May our lord instruct us and may he be granted a
  18. double reward by heaven.
