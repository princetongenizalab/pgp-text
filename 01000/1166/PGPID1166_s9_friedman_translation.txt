And all things that I possess and that I shall acquire, whether real estate or movables
will be surety and guarantees for the payment of this marriage contract.” And they stipulated between themselves con-
-cerning hate and love and life and death and all the court stipulations from beginning
to end. And they also stipulated between themselves that Ḥaẓiyya would live in Tyre twelve  
months, and afterwards he will move her to the city of Akko, without delaying or deferring.
Further they stipulated between themselves: If m(aster) Nathan, her father, should happen to go to Akko, after
his daughter Ḥaẓiyya goes (there), he will live in this house, whether he is alone or his wife  
is with him and his children, whether to visit or to dwell (there) all his life, because on this condition he had given it to his daughter
Ḥaẓiyya; and he had imposed on her this stipulation. Manṣūr, her husband, obligated himself by it without (any) protest 
and without hesitating. And the two sides took upon themselves the security and stringency of this  
deed according to the strength and stringency of all valid deeds, verified in court
not like promises nor like formularies of documents. And with this understanding, the two sides concluded  
and consented, not being coerced nor compelled; and they told the scribe  
to write and the witnesses to testify. And we performed with them a complete, strict qinyan,  
with an implement fit for doing this, for all that is written and specified above. Valid,  
correct, and verified is this marriage contract wherever produced and read, whether 
according to the statutes of our lords the sages or according to the laws of the gentiles. May he build and prosper. In good order.
The papers are three. There is written on the joint, perpendicularly, in two places "true and corr[ect]."
And the number of lines is ninety-four. Strong and firm.
