English Translation of Legal document: JCC 2

Richard Gottheil, "A Document of the Fifteenth Century concerning Two Synagogues of the Jews in Old Cairo," The Jewish Quarterly Review 18, no. 2 (n.p., 1927), 131-152.

  23. Now, these are at about one-third of the above-mentioned opening. The width of each buttress
  24. on its face is one cubit and one-eighth, according to the ordinary cubit.
  25. Its height, from the surface of the ground up to as far as that part which contains
  26. the laterals, is four cubits. Above this was something still higher...

  30. two restored pieces of marble. It is said that these are the two pillars which made up

  32. its upper part, so that the two aforementioned pillars had sagged. The height of the tower
  33. in addition to the top part of the building which had fallen into ruins was a cubit and a half.
  34. Now, throughout the whole length of the opening aforementioned there were laterals of wood
  35. resting upon wooden beams. In the middle
  36. of its elevation it reached two cubits—so that the destruction, taking account of the height of the tower, reached

  38. the two buttresses along the width of the buttresses. This computation is on the basis of the ordinary cubit (regarding)

  40. to that which they were before they were burned and to rebuild the building which had been destroyed to that which it had been
  41. as aforementioned. To the left, as one enters, is a roofed porch, the front porch of which
  42. has been destroyed up to three and a half cubits in length,
  43. and one and one-quarter cubits in width. Its depth has been uncovered
  44. one cubit by two. Its depth was a quarter of a cubit. The height
  45. needs a tearing down amounting to one extra cubit above the three and a half cubits.
  46. aforementioned. (It will be necessary) to rebuild this whole space from the bottom up three and a half to
  47. four cubits and a half. At the side (of this whole space) is another postern.
  48. Its height is two and a half cubits—all on the basis of the ordinary cubit.
  49. (It is necessary) to clean this up, to rebuild it and to restore it to its original form.
  50. In the condition in which it is now there is danger of injury occurring. Now, this Synagogue
  51. known as the Synagogue of the Rabbanites is in the Quarter of the Jews. (The whole property) can be described according to the four
  52. (cardinal points). The South reaches up to the little street in the Synagogue Enclosure
  53. which is there. In it there is the gate. A part of it belongs to the Waḳf of the Jews

  55. In this there is a wall about to tumble down. The Eastern Side reaches (unto)
  56. the small street in which is the aforementioned Synagogue, a part of which (belongs to the Waḳf)
  57. of the Jews. The western side reaches the waste ground in the aforementioned Synagogue Enclosure.
  58. Repairs in regard to this are not mentioned in the investigation. Now, as regards the Synagogue 
  59. known as that of the Palestinians—it is also known as the Synagogue of the Rabbanite Jews—
  60. it is inside the passage known as Khabīsah. It contains a façade built long ago.
  61. From it one enters the aforementioned Synagogue. And in the aforementioned Synagogue for one 
  62. who passes through it, there is a porch without a roof. In it are two pillars of marble; one of which to (the right)
  63. of the person as he enters. This is leaning to one side. It must be put in order and placed in the condition in which it was.
  64. In it is a large window, a part of which has already fallen, and a part of which is on the point of falling.
  65. Its size in breadth is three and one-third cubits, plus one quarter cubit; its height
  66. at the corner is two and a half cubits. On the middle of its side it is five cubits
  67. and two-thirds of a cubit. Now, for the space of a cubit from its lowest part up to the middle of it also
  68. needs rebuilding and restoring. In the aforementioned porch to the right of the reception room
  69. is a granite column in the corner; its measurement is in width two and one quarter cubits from its top
  70. to its bottom, in height three and one-half cubits and its restoration (is necessary)
  71. to the same amount in the mortar.  Now, in the aforementioned synagogue the roof is broken in and a door leads
  72. from it lying to the right of one who goes through it. From this door one enters into a place that is in ruins, one wall of which lies to the right as one enters.
  73. In it there is a door. The area needing repairs from the aforementioned door on is two and one third cubits.
  74. In height (this area) is two and one quarter cubits. Now, in the aforementioned chamber, the wall has fallen into ruins.
  75. It used to carry a tower. At the present time it is resting upon connecting beams. The extent of the wall
  76. aforementioned is two-thirds of a cubit in breadth and its height is five cubits. Next to it is a space needing repairs, the breadth of which
  77. is one-half a cubit and its height two and one-half cubits. All these cubits are the current cubits.
  78. All this stands in need of reconstruction and repair. If it is left in the condition in which it is at present, there may happen
  79. through this accidents. The following four boundaries will give the position in which this Synagogue lies.
  80. The south side reaches unto the market-place in the passage Khabīsah; the northern
  81. side reaches unto the street. In it is the gate (to the Synagogue). The eastern side
  82. reaches unto the house known as that of Ibn al-Muʿarrid and of al-ʿAṭṭār and of the others beside them. The western side
  83. reaches unto the dwelling compound known as the Hospice—a Waḳf for the poor of the Jews.
  84. This is what the investigation and the survey have clearly indicated. As for that which should be done in this matter
  85. that is left to that which the Mohammedan law may require. This occurred upon the blessed day (... the nine)
  86. and twentieth of the month Rabīʿa I in the year 878.
  87. Then, on the first day of Rabīʿa II in the aforementioned year, gave over ...
  88. the glorious, may Allah the most High grant them fame and give them strength and prolong the rule of their head-officer

  91. the final decision (?) to our excellent lord and master the chief Caḍi who is named above
  92. by his excellent name—May Allah prolong his flourishing days, do well by him and make his grandeur increase.
  93. He gave orders orally to go where are the two Synagogues mentioned above
  94. and to deal with this matter in harmony with the dictates of the holy law according to his excellent
  95. school (of law). Then went our lord and master, the chief Caḍi, who is mentioned
  96. by his excellent name above—May Allah the Most High lenghthen his days, give strength to his judgments and increase his dignity—
  97. accompanied by those of his colleagues who place their names at the end of this document, those stationed in the honored court of justice and others, 
  98. as well as the architects mentioned above, to where the two above mentioned Synagogues were.
  99. A thorough investigation was made of them in his presence, and the two were found to be in the condition mentioned
  100. above. Thereupon Allah was consulted—praised and exalted be He—who
  101. would not disappoint anyone consulting him; and h was asked of Him, whose kindness is never asked in vain—

  103. the learned Sheikh Taḳī-al-Dīn, the honoured one among the wise and (by) the most respected Mufti

  106. the two synagogues aforementioned and their ruins in a legal manner, 
  107. (made) by all those who were present at the first and second investigations, explained above.
  108. Now there was verified before our Master, the poor servant of Allah the Most High, the Sheikh Taḳī-al-Dīn

  111. above—a veritable legal decision on the fourth day of the month
  112. Rabīʿa II mentioned above in the aforementioned year. Then, after this

  114. the judge who is licensed (to deal with legal matters and) to whom reference has been made above—may Allah the Most High strengthen his judgments—

  116. the two Synagogues aforementioned. The restorations mentioned above
  117. were found to have been made and (the Synagogues) had been put into the same condition in which they had been (previous to)
  118. the destruction and burning. Nothing whatsoever had been added. It was found also that that which had been restored

  120. efore our master the wise man who has been referred to (above) may Allah the Most High confirm his judge—

  124. without any addition to this ... true, lawful, complete,
