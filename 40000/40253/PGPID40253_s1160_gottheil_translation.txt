according to the ordinary cubit. In it are two pedestals of marble erected long ago.
Now, these are at about one-third of the above-mentioned opening. The width of each buttress
on its face is one cubit and one-eighth, according to the ordinary cubit.
Its height, from the surface of the ground up to as far as that part which contains
the laterals, is four cubits. Above this was something still higher...
In the middle of the aforementioned opening is the aforementioned tower ...
the two buttresses aforementioned; and upon the two marble pillars (which were)
upon the two pedestals of marble aforementioned there are found the
two restored pieces of marble. It is said that these are the two pillars which made up

Now, throughout the whole length of the opening aforementioned there were laterals of wood
resting upon wooden beams. In the middle
of its elevation it reached two cubits—so that the destruction, taking account of the height of the tower, reached
three cubits and a half and an eighth of a cubit, taking into account the sagging ...
the two buttresses along the width of the buttresses. This computation is on the basis of the ordinary cubit (regarding)
the two aforementioned pillars up to that upon which they rested. And (it is necessary) to replace the towers
to that which they were before they were burned and to rebuild the building which had been destroyed to that which it had been

(It is necessary) to clean this up, to rebuild it and to restore it to its original form.
In the condition in which it is now there is danger of injury occurring. Now, this Synagogue

Repairs in regard to this are not mentioned in the investigation. Now, as regards the Synagogue 
known as that of the Palestinians—it is also known as the Synagogue of the Rabbanite Jews—
it is inside the passage known as Khabīsah. It contains a façade built long ago.
From it one enters the aforementioned Synagogue. And in the aforementioned Synagogue for one 
who passes through it, there is a porch without a roof. In it are two pillars of marble; one of which to (the right)
of the person as he enters. This is leaning to one side. It must be put in order and placed in the condition in which it was.
In it is a large window, a part of which has already fallen, and a part of which is on the point of falling.
Its size in breadth is three and one-third cubits, plus one quarter cubit; its height
at the corner is two and a half cubits. On the middle of its side it is five cubits
and two-thirds of a cubit. Now, for the space of a cubit from its lowest part up to the middle of it also
needs rebuilding and restoring. In the aforementioned porch to the right of the reception room
is a granite column in the corner; its measurement is in width two and one quarter cubits from its top
to its bottom, in height three and one-half cubits and its restoration (is necessary)
to the same amount in the mortar.  Now, in the aforementioned synagogue the roof is broken in and a door leads
from it lying to the right of one who goes through it. From this door one enters into a place that is in ruins, one wall of which lies to the right as one enters.
In it there is a door. The area needing repairs from the aforementioned door on is two and one third cubits.
In height (this area) is two and one quarter cubits. Now, in the aforementioned chamber, the wall has fallen into ruins.
It used to carry a tower. At the present time it is resting upon connecting beams. The extent of the wall
aforementioned is two-thirds of a cubit in breadth and its height is five cubits. Next to it is a space needing repairs, the breadth of which
is one-half a cubit and its height two and one-half cubits. All these cubits are the current cubits.
All this stands in need of reconstruction and repair. If it is left in the condition in which it is at present, there may happen
through this accidents. The following four boundaries will give the position in which this Synagogue lies.
The south side reaches unto the market-place in the passage Khabīsah; the northern
side reaches unto the street. In it is the gate (to the Synagogue). The eastern side
reaches unto the house known as that of Ibn al-Muʿarrid and of al-ʿAṭṭār and of the others beside them. The western side
reaches unto the dwelling compound known as the Hospice—a Waḳf for the poor of the Jews.
This is what the investigation and the survey have clearly indicated. As for that which should be done in this matter
that is left to that which the Mohammedan law may require. This occurred upon the blessed day (... the nine)
and twentieth of the month Rabīʿa I in the year 878.

and to deal with this matter in harmony with the dictates of the holy law according to his excellent
