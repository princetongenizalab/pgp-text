Take some of the ground lac with the sticks stripped away. Kn[ead it with some] of the
clarified bran water. Grind it in a mortar, as a paste, very finely until it becomes like 
an ointment. Extract it with the bran water through a sieve of many holes until 
the lac sediment runs clear. Take the silk that has been treated with forty 
dirhams of splintered alum after it has soaked in the alum and water for a night and a day, 
and proceed to wash off the alum and wring it. Take half of the water containing 
the lac extract and place it in a copper vessel. Light a fire beneath it until it boils and splits, 
then skim off the foam. Lower the silk into it and reduce the fire. Do not cease turning it
until it drinks up (the dye) and the water becomes clear. Take it out and rinse it in ample water, and do not. . . .
