English Translation of Letter: T-S 13J26.9

Marina Rustow, unpublished.

Recto

  1. I am writing, my master and lord, may God prolong your life and perpetuate your well-being and happiness, from Qayrawān at the end
  2. of the month of Elul in the year (4)44 (AH), in soundness of s[oul], praise be to God alone, having already sent
  3. several letters in whihc I infor[med you ...] and of what is still owed to you. Then there just now arrived
  4. your letter with the fayj dated to the second half of Sivan from which I learned of your well-being,
  5. may God perpetuate it for you. You mentioned that this year people's travel has been delayed. Likewise, here
  6. so far no one has proceeded to load any cargo. You mentioned the surfeit of goods from the west. Similarly, goods from
  7. the east have increased here, except for flax, because it is (already?) plentiful. As for sugar, today it's at one thousand dirha(ms) per
  8. qinṭ(ār). Sal ammoniac goes for one dīn(ār) and one-eighth per mann, brazilwood for 50 dīn(ārs), lac for 40 dīn(ārs). Good indigo is scarce, almost non-existent.
  9. Pepper is scarce and fine linen (sharāb) is expensive. You mentioned holding on to what remains of your flax. By my father's life, if
  10. I were able to sell it for you at the highest prices, I would be happy with that. What remains for you, my lord, in Qayrawān is
  11. two bales and a bolt (of cloth), and in Sūsa 12 bolts. The rest, the 35 bundles I sold at various prices, from
  12. 5 dīnārs to 4 and one-third at the lowest sale. Your letters had arrived saying not to buy merchandise for you,
  13. so I avoided the thing and improved most of it (?). I was hoping people would proceed to load the cargo so I could scheme to load it
  14. with the first people, but people delayed moment after moment until time slipped through their fingers. I will try
  15. if only I can manage something. You mentioned that I should buy oil and soap to trade with. I extend an apology: this
  16. year, I sought any means by which to send goods for you, but the only way to do this is for someone to travel to a place and load (cargo) from it
  17. bound for another place. You should know that there isn't a single merchant from among our associates available at the moment, but if I send (goods) with a group
  18. of gentiles to a place, if later they turn it around to travel to another country and we don't know them, it wastes time.
  19. God knows whether they'll get a ship or winter (there) or their intention will change to travel to a place we wouldn't choose (and then)
  20. your merchandise would be ruined. By my father's life, no (money) has been collected from the flax-sellers' market at this time except after several months.
  21. They scheme against the Muslims; all the more so against the Jews. I mixed (assets related to?) silk into it terribly,
  22. it (the money?) was received and "I buried it in the ground," scattered, away from the road. At the moment no one is shipping, and this month
  23. no one is shipping, and also because "(of what comes) from outside" (external threats?). With this, I see that you blame me when your money is present, which is better than your blaming me
  24. when your money is lost. The one who is present sees what the absent doesn't see. You mentioned carrying the sal ammoniac. May God facilitate its arrival
  25. for your salvation from a difficult man. You mentioned your concern and solicitude, may God reward you with good and grant you the best
  26. of His conditions. You mentioned carrying what remains in your account of the mixed-purse partnership (khulṭa) may God inscribe your (pl.) safety and unite you (pl.)
  27. with all of it. You mentioned buying clothing. I will buy them in winter, if God wills, and trade them as needed.
  28. Accept from me the best greetings. To my lord Abū Yaḥyā Nahray, the best greetings. To my lord the Kohen Abū Harūn, the best
  29. greetings. I will try to deliver the QWNH to his house, as you mentioned. You should know that my lord Abū Saʿīd
  30. son of Rabbi Moshe, (may his) m(emory be) b(lessed), and my master Abū l-Bishr Sulaymān son of Rabbi Faraḥ, (may his) m(emory be) b(lessed), have been in Sūsa since the month of
  31. Sivan. They've been selling there and receiving what is due to them, while I have been in Qayrawān since the month of Shevaṭ until now, dealing
  32. with the flax merchants, collecting from them. I have informed you of this so that you will know what those who send have to deal with and what
  33. those who don't send have to deal with. Also, I went to the gentile merchants here and bothered those I know
  34. and those I don't know to find one who will carry (goods) for me alone, but they exempted themselves from this. Even less than this is enough.
  35. I hope for good in this matter, and may the outcome be peace.

Verso

  1. This letter was delayed until the second half of the month. I've been informed that qāribs have proceeded to the loading stage in al-Mahdiyya,
  2. but I swear by my faith, I was unable to do anything (i.e., to load the cargo) because "\\The sword shall deal death// from without" (Deut. 32:25) and as for the cross, only days remain for it, while the qāribs are exposed.
  3. My soul didn't allow me to go out and reach many blockades and a heavy enemy at sea. By my father's life,
  4. my spirit has weakened on account of your things and the things of others, for they have become a trap for me. May God the exalted unite (me with) you (pl.) in His benificence.
  5. Represent me favorably before my lord Abū Zikrī Elḥanan. May the outcome be peace. I even, I swear by my father, went around to everyone
  6. carrying anything but found no possibility whatsoever. I spared no effort, I swear by my faith. Let's hope for the best.

Verso, address, right column

  1. To my lord and chief Abū Yaʿqūb Yūsuf b. ʿEli [Koh]en Fāsī, (may he) r(est in) E(den),
  2. may God prolong his life and perpetuate his wellbei[ng].

Verso, address, left column

  1. From his brother. May it arrive in Alexandria, God willing.
