Recto
That of which I inform you, my brother and my lord, may God keep him alive, preserve him, grant him [long] days,
and inscribe him in the Book of Life, is as follows: the culmination of our captivity and our exile, the ruin of our state, the destruction of our homes
and their desolation—may God seal (the account of) our sins. He has removed the crown of our head and gathered to the Garden of Eden the
excellent elder, the pious, the good and upright, the perfectly righteous, our teacher and master Barhūn son of master Moshe, of blessed memory.
[It suffices m]e to tell you that the death of him of blessed memory has fallen upon all of us, and our state is what
… and he departed to the Garden of Eden because of our sins, our humiliation, the shame of our faces, and our nakedness,
leaving a complete and perfect (legal) will. He appointed the executor over all his affairs and over his son, may he live forever,
be fruitful, multiply, teem like fish, and flourish, and may there be fulfilled in him (the verse) “The righteous man walks in his integrity; happy are his children after him”:
the excellent and illustrious elder, the pure, righteous one, the pious, the virtuous, the pure, the distinguished, the unique
... Yosef, may the Lord add (yosif) to his days and to his years and lengthen his days.
... He died on Friday in the forenoon (al-ḍuḥā), and was buried on the night of
Saturday, may one grow great from the house of a righteous man. That was on 8 Elul. The beauty of his death and burial
was such that others should take consolation from it. But we were deprived of mourning him. May God mend the hearts
of all of us, bar Moshe his son, may he live forever. I have informed you of this so that you may know how far the [?] have brought us
and to what point our condition has come. I have sent you, my lord, in his company, may he find mercy,
a [pur]se containing 1,424 pearls, valued at one and a half dinars. I ask you to contrive on my behalf
in disposing of them in whatever way you see fit, and to send me a little flax/linen from which we can make something to wear,
for I arrived here with nothing but a bakhla (?) of coarse sacking on my head and around my waist and a sandal on my foot. Let it be
... and ample at the chest, well made. (Send also) a little Ṭalḥī paper on which I can write to Baghdad and to the Land
[of Is]rael. I hope you will remember what passed between us there, and that you will act on it with resolve,
fulfil my wish in this matter, and send it to me with whichever of our associates you see fit.
I had informed you that everything I copied and that my father, may he rest in Eden, copied for me was carried off as spoil (subiyat) and lost
[and destroy]ed: “all who found them devoured them,” their enemies, Jews and gentiles alike. Because of this my
weakness of spirit has grown and I am helpless. Nothing remains, either in me or with me, with which to replace (i.e., copy) a single leaf,
and there is nowhere from which it could be replaced. The Torah has been effaced from the Maghrib at a single stroke. If anything remains, what remains is
something that cannot be relied upon, in the hands of people who have raised their heads while they do not know what the [
the measure of their condition. Whoever does not know his own state or sense his own worth — it would be best that he have
a teacher with him. Perhaps this is the End of Days, for everything the prophet of Israel warned of has come to pass, and more
besides. I would like your letter, by the courier (al-fayj), to set my mind at rest that you will carry out everything I have asked of you.
I have written in haste. We ask God to let us hear good news about Barhūn, your maternal aunt,
and her daughter on the government ship. May God place them in safety, God willing. Peace upon [   ].
