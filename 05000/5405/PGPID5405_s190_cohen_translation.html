English Translation of Letter: T-S 10J17.4

Mark R. Cohen, The Voice of the Poor in the Middle Ages (Princeton University Press, 2005).

Recto

I(n the name of the) M(erciful)

Your slave Joseph b. ʿAllān

the Alexandrian 

  1. kisses the ground before the [l]ofty, most illustrious seat of the master
  2. R. Ḥananel the wise and discerning, son of his honor, greatness and holiness (our) ma(ster)
  3. and t(eacher) Samuel the precious elder, the munificent benefactor, may his end be better than
  4. his beginning and may he live a long life to see
  5. living offspring from you, and may He make your own reputation a good one in this world and the next. 
  6. I inform you that I am from a good family. My father is one of the
  7. India traders, and God favored us. Your father knows
  8. my father and brother Futūḥ, who recently went on a journey to Yemen
  9. and died on shipboard. The elder Maḍmūn took his money
  10. and transferred it to his heirs in the port (Alexandria). When he (my brother) was setting out on his travels he made a bequest of something
  11. to me, if his time should come, namely, that I should be paid something
  12. out of his estate. But no docum[ent] to t[h]is effect was properly executed and recorded,
  13. for we were drinking at the time. Your slave went up (to Fustat)
  14. to appeal to his excellency our lord, may his glory be exalted.
  15. The latter wrote a number of letters to the community on my account,
  16. hoping I would be provided with something to take back to support my family, which consists of nine
  17. souls. Now that I am starving and thirsty and
  18. without clothing or anything else, I ask God and you
  19. to "cover" me with a robe so I can enter the synagogue to hear "Kadosh"
  20. and "Barukh" in the presence of the noble God, having in the past been used to wearing "niṣāfī" and "ṣābūrī"

Recto, right margin

  1. and "mutakhkhat." May He save me from a cycle (of misfortune). And may your welfare increase.
