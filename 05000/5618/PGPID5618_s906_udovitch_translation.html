English Translation of Letter: Bodl. MS heb. d 75/20

A. L. Udovitch, "Formalism and informalism in the Social and Economic institutions of the Medieval Islamic World," in Individualism and Conformity in Classical Islam (Harrassowitz, 1977), 61–81.

Recto

  1. May God prolong the life of my lord and master and preserve your wellbeing and happiness and, in his mercy, deter all evil from you.
  2. I am writing to you from Alexandria on the 10th day of Kislev—may God conclude it for us and for you in the most favorable manner.
  3. I am well and long for you; and I pray that God will bring about a meeting between us in the best of circumstances. You probably would like to know [the following]:
  4. Since I left here to travel to Syria I have received only the following letters from you: One letter addressed to Jerusalem and one letter here.
  5. My own letters to you since my departure would not have been so long delayed had it not been for my persistent physical exhaustion.
  6. I arrived in Tyre and spent only five days doing business in the place and remained twenty days confined to the bed.
  7. God then bestowed health upon me and I departed for Jaffa and from there went up to Jerusalem, may God rebuild it. There, as well, I did not do any business,
  8.  except for eight days and remained for an entire month confined to bed with chills and fever. And, by God, I was not able to ascend
  9. the mountain except by riding on a mount. My spirit was very discouraged. But God, for his name's sake, bestowed his favor upon me and gave me
  10. health and I departed from the house, while still in a slightly weak condition: I experienced great hardship in my crossing to Tinnis" and from Tinnis
  11. to Alexandria, hardships which it would take too long to detail. I give praise to God who brought my travel to a favorable conclusion and returned me safely.
  12. When I returned here, I was apprised of news which stunned me and shook my very being. I refer to the death of the elder Abū Isḥaq
  13. Barhūn, may he find mercy. I was overcome by a distress which I am unable to describe. May God have mercy upon him
  14.  and put his posterity in his place; for there is no turning away the decree of God. Yesterday, I also learned of your losses
  15. during the current year. I was very distressed at this news, but it could have been worse,
  16. and this is not a time for despair. For cspecially this year, there is no one who
  17. has not suffered a loss. May God quickly replace [the loss] for you and for us and consider what has happened as a ransom for your soul.
  18. and grant you and us blessings in all that follows. And may he privilege me to see you with a male heir. My master,
  19. the letter of my cousin Abii Sa'id Sahnan16 has arrived. In it he mentions that upon his arrival in Tripoli he found the community
  20. suffering under a heavy yoke. The Sultan has imposed a fine upon them. Consequently, he was not able to speak to him [i.e., the Sultan] upon his
  21. arrival in the matter which you know about. However, he will not neglect this matter during this winter.
  22. [He also mentioned] that there was no opportunity to sell the lapis lazuli which belongs to our partnership, for I am very busy this winter, and [when it is sold]
  23. he [i.e., my cousin] will send the price it fetches either by land or by sea. May God grant him his favor. I have already informed you, O my master, that I have sent
  24. in the care of Mūsā as-Sfaqsī on the Nile boat of Ibn Khallūf aṣ-Ṣaghīr a bundle of linen
  25. upon which it is written: Mūsā b. Abī l-Ḥayy Khalīla. I have hired this transportation for it from him for a quarter-dinar. I have already paid it to him,
  26. and there remains nothing further to pay for this bundle. In the middle of this bundle you will find a note detailing its contents.
  27. If God grants its safe arrival, would my master please take delivery of it and then transfer it from him [i.e., Mūsā as-Sfaqsī] to my home.
  28. If possible, would you please try to sell in Cairo the garment-sized cloths (firākh) which are in the bundle; sell [no more] than ten at a time.
  29. Please do this, if you can; however, if you are not free to do so, I have asked Abū ʿAlī Ḥassūn.

Verso

  1. I have already mentioned that R. Yeshuʿa intends to travel to Būṣīr.  If he should do so before my arrival [to Fustat], please give him
  2. 100 dinars so that he can make purchases for me with them. He has already promised me to do so. When, God willing, I arrive, and if it seems wise to send additional money, then please send it to him.
  3. I wrote to him instructing him concerning the purchases he should make for me. As for myself, I will, God willing, not delay [in coming].
  4. From Ascalon, I have already sent to you in a bundle despatched by the elder Abu Isḥaq Abraham son of the scholar
  5. a Ṭabarī sofa [cover] consisting of two pieces and a muʿayyan (?) / muʿīn cloak. I have also sent a piece of fabric with a man called Sahl. I hope
  6. to receive your letter confirming the arrival of all these shipments in the near future. I have also asked the elder Abu Isḥaq to buy two veils for me 
  7. from Ascalon. I have asked him to send them to you and I hope that he has already done this. The bundle of linen which I sent with
  8. Ibn Khallūf, does not have a certificate from the Dār Mānak. In my absence, the elder Abū Isḥaq addressed it [i.e., the shipment]
  9. in the name of Isḥaq b. Khalaf since that is what was written upon the bundle. I protested that its designation should be changed and I had it changed back to my name,
  10. for fear of the deterioration of the times. If God sees to its safe arrival, please, pay its fee at the Dār Mānak for me,
  11. and put it, God willing, on my account with you. I would like to inform you, my master, that ... the letter ... and I
  12. do not know the courier. He has already departed and had the opportunity ... with Abū ʿAlī Ḥassūn ... two bags of coins ... one of them
  13. good rubāʿīs. [Most of the remainder of this line is illegible.] ... that the letter
  14. would be in the possession of Abū ʿAlī Ḥassūn, but he was not able to depart, may God facilitate his affairs. God willing, I will not delay my departure
  15. beyond this Friday. I need not stress the matter of the garment-sized cloths. If God sees to their safe arrival,
  16. would you please make an effort to sell them for me for ready cash, before my arrival [in Fustat.] God willing, it will then
  17. be easy for me to obtain the proceeds. Abū ʿAlī Ḥassūn has promised to help you with respect to collecting the money from [these sales.]
  18. Please write to me quickly confirming their arrival as well as the two bags of coins in the possession of Abū ʿAlī Ḥassūn b. Yaḥyā
  19. and the bag of coins in the possession of Mūsā as-Sfaqsī. Make out the address of the letter to my brother. For, if it arrives while I am still
  20. here, I will see it, and if it arrives after my departure, my brother will receive it. By this you will be doing me a favor. I have added these lines
  21. in haste since the courier is about to depart, and I fear that he may leave without my seeing him. I have informed you of this, and please accept my apologies.
  22. If my departure is delayed, and someone else from here leaves before I do, I will send a letter with him containing full details. I send you
  23. my best regards and to my master the illustrious Rabbi. my best regards. Best regards to your brothers-in-law. Regards to the Levite, and regards
