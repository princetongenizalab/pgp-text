English Translation of Letter: T-S 10J12.1

Oded Zinger, "Narrating Masculine Failure: The Marital Woes of a Twelfth-Century Jewish Physician in Egypt," Al-Masāq Journal of the Medieval Mediterranean April (Taylor and Francis, 2026), 1–19.

  1. In (Your) Name, O Merci(ful),
  2. (This is) my letter to my father, dearest to me of all people. You are to me the father substituting for a father to the point that
  3. there is not a single time that a letter reaches me from you, without it being like 
  4. I am seeing your face. May God unite us before our parting from this world.
  5. As for the reason why I have not been writing to you regularly, it boils down to two reasons. The first is
  6. the onslaught of fate and its vicissitudes, (with which I do not bother you) so that your heart will not be grieved, let alone
  7. your health. (The second reason is to prevent) the proclamation of my hiding for your sake. In short,
  8. your slave substantiates what God said to his prophet: “O, mortal, I am about to take away from you
  9. the delight of your eyes through pestilence and the word of the Lord came to me in the morning and in the evening my wife died”. (Ezekiel 24:15-18)
  10. When it was thus decreed upon me, I became perplexed because she was to me a “support from the town” (Samuel II 18:3).
  11. She left behind for me this 3-year-old boy and I became dependent on people on his account
  12. after he relied (on others’ help) and reunited (with me). As soon as I left the town after 18 days, slandered, I
  13. and four other physicians (who came) in my honour, I was overtaken by expenses, for cloths,
  14. for shrouds, burial and reception. For she was dear to her people and the town, (totalling) 12 dinars!
  15. While we were fulfilling her rights, we returned and found that everything in the house was carried away 
  16. and I had to return everything myself. After I was single for one year and sick for three years,
  17. I lost everything I had. God decreed that I entered
  18. al-Maḥalla and met Ibn Sabra, (who) are today my in-laws, to obtain 
  19. his opinion on my illness. We spoke about the matter of this woman and agreed that the maintenance
  20. would be on me. I married her for various reasons: one of which was their 
  21. respectability, dignity and knowledge of medicine. The other reason is that I wanted my child to be well brought up and that I would not
  22. be dependent on people. The other reason is that I have (already) studied all that I could from his expertise.
  23. For this learning (of medicine) is difficult for me due to (my) old age and the yoke of house (i.e. wife) and kingship.
  24. Her family believed that I (still) had with me a remainder (of my wealth). However, fate ruined me and what
  25. was in my hand. Remorse seized her and she started lifting from the house one item after
  26. another until she emptied the house to an amount of one hundred and fifty dinars.
  27. When I demanded its return, I told her “Renounce your delayed marriage payment” but she refused and it amounted to
  28. twenty dinars. I became angry and removed my child from them and returned him to his family. 
  29. I kept away from them for two years in Minyat Ghamr, Alexandria and in the Buḥayra province.
  30. It was decreed that someone gave me a lift to Abyār. The Sheikh had sent
  31. Abū al-Hayjāʾ al-Samīn towards me

recto, margin

  1. so send me his income. I had, in my own hand, a legal opinion (fatwā) from the Rayyis R. Moshe that it is she who is rebellious,
  2. I do not owe her anything! While I was away, she gave birth to a girl of mine. The girl grew and she weaned her, while I was away. I went back to al-Maḥalla 
  3. and we went to court. The telling of it all would take too long. (In the end,) she brought back all the household items while I returned the boy. Between one thing and another, in this situation,
  4. I spent out of my hand, from what money I obtained, 15 dinars. They did this, easing my heart for a few days. However, they returned to removing (from the house) item after item and selling them.

verso

  1. My life worsened further. She was arrogant to me through this boy of mine and through the baby girl,
  2. who I maintained at my expense. (My) livelihood in al-Maḥalla dwindled.
  3. I became irritated (lit. my chest tightened) as my fortunes with women turned upside down. For I am looking at a house (meaning: a wife) who is completely not worth anything.
  4. For my house, in this shape, is unfit for a friend or an enemy.
  5. I entered the little one into a school (kuttāb) because he is seven years old today. I do not have with me
  6. a Bible (muṣḥaf). Because of the thing I told you about, if you have something with you that will show his (i.e. the writer’s 7-year-old son) favour with you 
  7. from the days of the deceased, M(ay his) M(emory be blessed), (give it) as a gift to Abū al-Ḥasan (i.e. the writer’s son). For he is (like) your own son to the point that he
  8. asks me for you and always says to me “my lord, show me my (paternal) uncle!” I did not know
  9. anything afflicted you until Abū al-Surūr met me and told me what happened
  10. to you with Yaḥyā, the Elder, may he be remembered for his evil deeds by the divine law whose
  11. power we all know. It saddened me, as whenever something is revealed about one of us,
  12. and especially you! May God, the exalted, punish him and may he not shame us by his reputation
  13. more than already took place. What happened to you with him will have no effect. Moreover, you will go with him
  14. to the military commander, (let) someone else punish him! And now, my uncle, I do not
  15. have any other pain that you (would) want to see, more than this matter. I am 
  16. in a state of fear with these people (i.e. the Ibn Sabra family) for I want to raise him and how will God help me
  17. to be free from them? For they have made me grow old before my time! How I threw
  18. myself down like a riding animal (for them)! I did not rush to Fustat to procure in it what I want,
  19. instead, I have taken from the countryside the things I needed like “Harvest is past etc”. I ask from
  20. your kindness that you will not inform him of my situation nor where I am; for if he will arrive
  21. at my location, there will be shame among Jews. These people and I are in
  22. such a situation! So strive to remove the suspicions about me. I also sent a letter
  23. to inform my first in-laws that they should not inform him where I am. Send me (a letter)
  24. informing me what happens with him, whether he is in the city or outside of it. Perhaps
  25. he would encounter on the road what others encountered and we will not be punctilious with him.
  26. In short, (tell me) everything that happens to him for he is my link to (what) I had in my previous life: he and his mother.
  27. With them (i.e. the Ibn Sabra family) are fifty dinars. They did not come to an agreement with me about what they have (of mine) at all. Nor did they acknowledge
  28. anything (i.e. any debt) to me. While I carried them (at my expense) as if they were guests! The old and the young treated them with honour
  29. out of respect to me. After that, she joined him, obeyed him and went
  30. behind him. She did not die with me. A son of Israel buried her, may God attach her to Him.

verso, margin

  1. I am sending you greetings and to the Elder Abū al-Munajjā send him my greetings and to the elder Abū ʿAlī and your children and their little ones. Praise
  2. to the One who decreed this situation as well as what follows for us. God, the exalted, grant what is best. Peace on to you and to all of
  3. Israel, Amen.
