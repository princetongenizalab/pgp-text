English Translation of Letter: Bodl. MS heb. a 2/20

S. D. Goitein, Letters of Medieval Jewish Traders (Princeton University Press, 1973).

Recto

I am writing to you, my lord and master—may God prolong your life and make permanent the days of your well-being, happiness, and prosperity—on Tuesday, 26th of the month of Elul (September).

I am well and prosperous—praise to God in gratefulness—and longing to see you. May God bring us together soon.

I have sent you a number of letters from al-Mahdiyya and Sfax and hope they have arrived and you have read them. I informed you in them that I have dispatched two purses to you from al-Mahdiyya with Abū Yaʿqūb Yūsuf al-Dūkī, one for our partnership and one for my own account. The first contained 50 dinars eastern currency; and also 57 mithqals, gold ore and old gold, worth 50 dinars, totaling 100 dinars. I hope it has arrived. I also sent you 50⅔ dinars eastern currency for my own account; furthermore, 63 Ḥasanī dinars—may God ordain their safe arrival.

In the ship of the Amir Nāṣir al-Dawla I sent you thirty-one new large skins of oil, measuring sixty-two maṭars; on their stoppers the names Salama and Judah are engraved. They are closed with small stoppers and fixed tightly with soaked palm leaves and parchment. With them are two leather bags with soap, weighing five qinṭārs. The names Judah and Salama are engraved on these as well. I hope everything will arrive. It is all in the hands of ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAlī b. Ḥabīb Sfaqsī. The balance of the amount advanced for the whole partnership comes to 1¼ dinar.

In the same ship, I sent you two purses with ʿUmar b. ʿUthmān al-Qa(z)dīrī Sfaqsī, one for the account of the partnership, containing 20 Shaykhiyya dinars, worth 18⅓ , and one for my own account, in which there are 20 dinars of full weight of eastern currency. Please add this to the first purse, sent with al-Dūkī.

Together with this letter, I am sending you for my own account a purse in which there are 44 Ḥasanī dinars, weighing 43 dinars, as well as large dinars of eastern currency, 56 in number, and Ḥākimī rubāʿīs, 20 in number, of a total weight of 60 dinars.

Please sell the HasanI dinars of the two shipments for me and buy shining flax of first quality for the dinars belonging to me, if possible, Māwī or Mīsārī, unique in excellence, and if you find real Fayyumī, knife-edge blades, buy whatever you can—I ask God to inspire you with successful ideas.

For our joint business, too, make an effort and buy whatever you see fit of flax and spices. You will find a list of the prices of the spices in my letter to Ḥassūn. Please make an effort and buy accordingly both for our joint business and for me personally. Success, however, lies in God's hands. Send everything at the beginning of the sailing season to Sfax. When you find an opportunity to send flax to Sicily, do so and address it to Isḥāq b. Khalaf. Don't be stingy to write, but list in detail everything you received and sent, so that I shall be able to act according to your letters.

I asked you in my previous letters to buy me a flame colored robe, which should be short and well fitting of fine, not coarse, material; I would like to wear it first on the Day of Atonement. Likewise, a turban of fine sharb-linen, which should be short and of pleasant, not coarse, material. Send with this some odorous wood, flasks (of camphor) and perfume, with which I can appear before my lord, the qāʾid—may God give him victory. Deduct everything from the sum due to me.

I sent you and to the elder M. Abū Sḥaq Ibrāhīm, with the Kohen al-Dūkī, 100 small sheets of ... as "soap for your service." They cost a little over 9 dinars; details are included in the letter sent with them. I hope everything will arrive.

Recto, right margin

I wrote to your brother in Tripoli urging him to forward those Aleppan shuqqa-gowns which he had there in Tripoli. I hope he has got them.

I wrote this letter hurriedly; kindly excuse its form. Greetings to you, your boys, and brothers-in-law, as well as to everyone inquiring about me. And Peace.

Recto, top margin

Regards to M. Abū Yaḥyā Nahray. Inform him that I sent him the balance due to him with the oil through ʿAbd al-Raḥmān. I hope it will arrive.

Verso, address, right column

To my master and lord Abū Zikrī—may God prolong his life—Ibn Sighmār—may God be his protector, the dear, R. Judah, son of R. Moses, (may he) r(est in) E(den),

Verso, address, left column

From his friend Mūsā, son of Isaac, (may he) r(est in) E(den) Sfaqsī.
