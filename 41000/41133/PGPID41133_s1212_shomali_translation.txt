The validity of this document was confirmed before me. It was written by Ḥasan ibn ʾIbrāhīm ibn Rizq in his (own) handwriting on its date.
In the name of God, the Compassionate, the Merciful
This is what Rabābīl ibn Mīnā ibn Ǧirǧah al-Ṭuṭūni has bought from Qarhīwah and Sarīh the daughters of Ǧirǧah the son of Shanūdah and they are both [numbered]
Among the inhabitants of Ṭuṭūn, one of the villages in the district of Fayyūm. He bought from them both their share and their inheritance from their father, Ǧirǧah ibn Shanūdah,
This being four shares less an eighth of a share from the twenty-four shares, held in common, not divided between them and their brothers,
Mīnā ibn Ǧirǧah and his brother, Suwayrus. Rabābīl ibn Mīnā ibn Ǧirǧah bought these four shares less an eighth of a share of the house
known as the house of Biyāq to its limit and its boundaries, with its entrance and exit, and every right within it
and every [right] outside it, and what is enclosed by [its] four boundaries. The first boundary, and that is the southern one extends to the house of Ḥasan.
Its second boundary, that is the northern boundary, extends to the spacious house known as the house of the bishop.
Its third boundary, that is the western one, extends to the pavilion which belongs to Ṭāhir ibn Ḥasan ibn Rizq. And its
fourth boundary, that is the eastern one, is (formed by) the thoroughfare to which the door (of the property) gives access. The entrance and the exit (of the property) are from this (side).
He bought all of this property with its timbers, doors, and all its facilities (i.e. washroom and kitchen) for two dīnārs and fourteen carats
in gold coins, of full weight and good alloy, from the ones customary in Egypt and its administrative districts. Qarhīwah received
this price, along with her sister Sarīh, in full and completely. And they were released of the (ownership of this) property by means of a receipt (acknowledging that) they have received and taken over (the price) fully.

And they delivered it to Rabābīl ibn Mīnā. And he received it, and acquired it, and possessed it. It became part of his wealth and among his properties.

If he wishes he may sell it. If he wishes he can give it away as gift. If he wishes he may reside in it. Whatever arises by way of an attachment, right due, a creditor
or someone having right to an inheritance, then the execution of that and its clearing is the obligation of Gharhīwah and her sister Sīrah, the daughters of Ǧirǧah ibn Shanūdah

From her own property whatever it may amount to. [They performed this transaction] while being sound in mind and body, legally capable of conducting their affairs, acting willingly, not forced

nor constrained, and while being pleased with it. This document was written in the month Rabīʿ al-ʾĀkhar of the year four hundred and five.
