Recto
May God prolong the life of my brother and dear master, and shield you, [T1 adds: preserve you, watch over you,] protect you, guard you, and make His bounty to you last.
I am writing to you on the eve of the Fast of Kippur (may God accept it from us, from you, and from all Israel, with forgiveness and good pleasure)
in safety, health and well-ordered circumstances, according to the best of the Creator’s customary favors (may He be exalted and glorified) and the greatest of His benefits,
we and all our circle, abundant praise be to Him. Likewise your son Ṣedaqa, may God keep him, and the household (al-ahl), and all
those you left behind are in the best state you know of, and better. Your son, may God guard him, grows and prospers every
day. He has turned into a grown man, a generous (bādhil; T1 kāzil?) merchant, and one may hope he will succeed you and add to your standing (al-jāh, T1; damaged in T2).
I had already sent you letters with Abū Yaʿqūb Yūsuf b. Maṭrūḥ, may God keep him, in which I explained to you
everything you need to know about [your] affairs, and what had reached me in the way of a letter from Ṣedaqa’s father/mother (wālid/wālidat Ṣedaqa) or [ … ]
his son Abū l-Khayr, may God keep him. I spoke about this (T1 takallamtu; T2 tasallamtu(?), “I received”) and copied out (?) the petition/account (al-qiṣṣa), and [reconciled(?)] the parties (al-nufūs, T2)
with one another, but it cannot be completed except in your presence, may God guard you. I hope that will be
soon and that He will make it easy for you. I had written to you that you should do with whatever remains with you of the
shipment (al-ḥiml) whatever you think proper and profitable, and then set out with it. I hope you have done so.
I asked you, my brother and master, to buy me a quarter [raṭl?] of camphor, shirbazī (?), of the highest, finest quality; and of
Indian tutty (tūtiyā; T1 halīlaj(?), myrobalan), genuine and fine, two mithqāls of the white [kind], the kind [that comes] in shells, the shells of
ostrich eggs (T2 bayḍ al-naʿām; T1 bayḍ al-Shām); and also of the good, fine green [kind], because I need to use it for my eyes;
and of fine paper (al-kāghad al-laṭīf) like this kind, 5,000 good sheets; and of ṭalḥī [paper], 1,000 sheets of top quality.
As for the needs my earlier letter mentioned, which I have forgotten and not listed this
time, you, may God keep you, need no one to instruct you about them. As for Abū l-Surūr, he had
a good (T2 ḥasana; T1 sāra, “pleasing”) and lucrative (mujbira) journey. Someone whose word I trust told me (T2 qīla lī; T1 qāla lī) that he is making a dinar per dinar in wheat (T2 al-qamḥ; T1 al-qumāsh, “cloth”) [interlinear] on this
journey, since it was blessed for him, praise be to God; may God increase him in every good. My brother, I
charged you with the matter of Abū Zikrī b. al-Aqṭaʿīnā (?), may God keep him: that you meet with him about what
we have with him and spare [us] the trouble in this. We have not seen a letter from you about it, so do not neglect it.
[ … ] in this world, it would be better for him, more decent and more upright. I have advised [ … ]
[ … ] the Creator, exalted and glorified, upon him. Do not delay your letter regarding all matters whatsoever.
[ … ] and inform [me] of them. I have written in haste, at the time the people are boarding the ship (al-markab, T2; lacuna in T1)
this day. May God ease [the voyage] for them and decree their safety and the safety of all Israel, amen.
Peace be upon you, and God’s mercy and blessing. The boys (al-ṣibyān), all of them, and your brothers [send] greetings.
They are in Qayrawān; they celebrated the holiday (ʿayyadū) with their families in the best of circumstances, may God keep them abundantly.
(T2 only:) And from [ … ]
