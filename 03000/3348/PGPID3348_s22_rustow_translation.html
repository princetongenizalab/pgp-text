Unspecified (Unpublished Transcriptions) Translation of Letter: T-S 13J23.14

Marina Rustow, unpublished.

Recto

  1. May God prolong the life of my brother and dear master, and shield you, protect you, watch over you, guard you, and make your benefactions eternal.
  2. I am writing to you on the eve of the Fast of Kippur, may God accept it from us, from you, and from all Isr(ael) with forgiveness and good pleasure,
  3. in well-being, health and well-ordered circumstances, according to the best of the Creator’s customary favors, may He be exalted and glorified, and the greatest of His benefits,
  4. we and the rest of our community, abundant praise be to Him. Likewise, your son Ṣadaqa, may God keep him, and the household, and all
  5. those you left behind are in the best state you know of, and better. Your son, may God guard him, grows and prospers every
  6. day. He has turned into a grown man, a generous merchant, and one may hope he will succeed you and add to your jāh.
  7. I had already sent you letters with Abū Yaʿqūb Yūsuf b. Maṭrūḥ, may God keep him, in which I explained to you
  8. everything you need to know about (your) affairs, and what had reached me by way of a letter from Ṣadaqa’s mother or [ … ]
  9. his son Abū l-Khayr, may God keep him. I received and had someone copy out the petition and asked ...
  10. with each other, but it can be completed only in your presence, may God guard you. I hope that will be
  11. soon and that He will make it easy for you. I had written to you that you should do with whatever you still have from the
  12. cargo (al-ḥiml) whatever you think proper and profitable, and then set out with it. I hope you have done so.
  13. I asked you, my brother and master, to buy me a raṭl of Sharbuzī camphor, of the highest, finest quality; and of
  14. Indian tutty, genuine and fine, two mithqāls of the white kind, the kind that comes in shells, the shells of
  15. ostrich eggs; and also of the good, fine green kind, because I need to use it for my eyes;
  16. and of fine paper like this kind, 5,000 good sheets; and of ṭalḥī [paper], 1,000 sheets of top quality.
  17. As for the requests my earlier letter mentioned, which I have forgotten and not listed this
  18. time, you, may God keep you, need no one to instruct you about them. As for Abū l-Surūr, he had
  19. a good and lucrative journey. Someone whose word I trust told me that he is making a dinar per dinar \\in wheat/cloth (Goitein and Gil give different readings)// on this
  20. journey, since it was blessed for him, praise be to God. May God increase him in every good. My brother, I
  21. charged you with the matter of Abū Zikrī b. al-Aqṭaʿīnā (?), may God keep him: that you meet with him about what
  22. we have with him and spare (us) the trouble in this. We have not seen a letter from you about it, so do not neglect it.
  23. [ … ] in this world, it would be better for him, more decent and more upright. I have advised [ … ]
  24. [ … ] the Creator, exalted and glorified, upon him. Do not delay your letter regarding all matters whatsoever.
  25. [ … ] and inform [me] of them. I have written in haste, at the time the people are boarding the ship
  26. this very day. May God ease (the voyage) for them and decree their safety and the safety of all Israel, amen.
  27. Peace be upon you, and God’s mercy and blessing. The boys, all of them, and your brothers [send] greetings.
  28. They are in Qayrawān; they celebrated the holiday with their families in the best of circumstances, may God keep them abundantly.
  29. From [ … ]

Verso, address, right column

  1. To my brother and lord Abū Ibrāhīm, may God prolong his life ...
  2. ʿAyyāsh b. Nissīm b. Barūkh, may God keep him. 

Verso, address, left column

  1. From Yehuda b. Yosef, may he rest in Eden.
