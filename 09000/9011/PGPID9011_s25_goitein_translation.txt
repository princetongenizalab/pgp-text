On Thursday, the 21st of Adar,
in the year 1409 of the era according to which we are accustomed to reckon
in Fustat of Egypt, which is situated on the Nile river, the following occurred:
There appeared before the court, instituted by his honor, his greatness, his holiness, our teacher, our master,
our lord Mevorakh, Chief of Discernment, Sage of the Yeshiva, (member of the) Great Sanhedrin, Prince
of Princes, Strength of the House of Israel, may his glory increase, M. Sason b. Natan, and M. Moshe
ha-Levi b. Moshe. M. Sason claimed that M. Moshe
had come with him to his storehouse and asked to be offered a basket of antimony; he opened it and looked at it,
\\and the price was fixed at five dinars for one Laythī qinṭār, payable after four months.// (Moshe) called a weigher and porters, but while the basket was transported,
it burst, whereupon M. Moshe said: "There is much earth in this basket. How much reduction do you grant me?"
I (Sason) said to him: "I have bought a number of such baskets; I shall grant you the same reduction as that made in my favor by the representative of the merchants."
Upon this, (Moshe) said: "I bought \\antimony// through Ben Salmān, the broker, and he reduced the price by the value of thirty
pounds' weight per basket." I (Sason) replied that I agreed to accept the evaluation of Abū ʿAlī b. Salmān; whatever he said,
I would accept. Then (Moshe), after having divided the load into two baskets and having them transported away by porters,
was met by Ḥusayn al-Dimashqī, who asked to be offered some of the antimony; he (Moshe) gave him a sample and, winking(?)
his eye, said to him: "It was bought \\from that man//, twenty (pounds) for a dinar." On the following day, M. Moshe came to me (Sason)
and asked me to fetch Abū ʿAlī, that he should evaluate the reduction. I (Sason) brought Abū ʿAlī
to his house (dār), and in the presence of Isḥāq b. Ḥunayn, Abū ʿAlī evaluated the antimony
and ruled that the reduction should be fifteen, but (Moshe) swore that he agreed solely to thirty, whereupon I (Sason) said
that I would grant only what the broker had fixed. I (Sason) waited until the stipulated time of payment, which I
now demand from him. Asked about this, M. Moshe declared,
"I agreed to buy the antimony for the price
mentioned. However, at the time of the weighing, the basket burst, and I saw the antimony,
\\and its weight was three hundred and eighty-four pounds."//
